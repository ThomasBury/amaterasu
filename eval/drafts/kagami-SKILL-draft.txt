# Kagami — Reflect claims against reality.

Give a useful, scoped assessment of communication, never a rating of its author.
This is an unvalidated reasoning aid, not an automated truth detector.

## Workflow

1. Get separate authority before sending, filing, publishing, or changing
   external records. Accept a URL, pasted post, article, or paper. Retrieve
   the supplied material first with available host tools, or read the pasted
   text or supplied file. This is separate from searching for corroboration.
   Apply shared essentials 5–6 to both. Record source identity, locations,
   context, and review date. Establish access as complete, partial, or
   unavailable. For blocked pages, request pasted text or an export and
   continue with accessible material without reconstructing missing content.
   For papers, state which portions you inspected. An abstract cannot
   establish uninspected methods or results.
2. Read [evidence and arguments](references/evidence-and-arguments.md).
   Classify each consequential passage as a clear proposition, vague but
   repairable, or having no determinate proposition; quote the last two and
   ask only relevant resolving questions. Never strengthen vague wording into
   a claim attributed to its author.
3. Extract the smallest independently assessable claims. Inspect supplied
   evidence first, including supplied comparison sources; record source
   identity, author, date, location, and narrow relationship. Evaluate meaning,
   support, and inference separately, including assumptions, exceptions,
   qualifications, and plausible alternatives.
4. For numbers or product claims, also read
   [numbers and product claims](references/numbers-and-product-claims.md).
   Recompute simple arithmetic with available tools; show units, denominators,
   and formula. Correct arithmetic does not verify the measurements.
5. For public research, follow the search budget in evidence and arguments:
   at most six counted queries, discovery first when requested and permitted,
   up to three consequential unresolved claims with at most two targeted
   searches each. Inspect original sources; search snippets and model memory
   are not inspected evidence. Further research requires an explicit user
   request. For requested comparison, follow the coverage-comparison procedure
   in the same reference.
6. For promises, apply the compact promise check in evidence and arguments:
   actor, promised outcome, deadline, conditions, and dated evidence of
   fulfillment as of the review date. A promise to recommend is not a promise
   of approval. Do not invoke another skill.
7. Assign evidence statuses only where applicable, using the status table in
   evidence and arguments: not examined is **not checked**; attempted but
   unresolved is **insufficient evidence**. Call empty wording and misleading
   reasoning out plainly; explain the actual inference error rather than merely
   naming a fallacy. Unsupported does not mean false. Explicitly recognize
   substantive, well-supported material, including a review with no material
   problems.
