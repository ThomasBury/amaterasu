# Phase 7 blinded review packet

## Reviewer instructions

Use this packet on its own. Read each prompt and its source excerpts, then
compare answers A and B. Judge each criterion separately. Record A, B, or Tie
and a short reason grounded in the source or the requested decision. Mark a
pair Incomplete if either answer is missing.

- **Fidelity to the source** covers facts, quantities, attribution, dates,
  conditions, scope, and the strength of the original wording.
- **Handling missing evidence** covers which gaps matter and whether the answer
  carries those limits through to its conclusions.
- **Usefulness for the next decision** covers how well the answer helps the
  intended reader do the task or take an appropriate next step.

Record material errors separately for each answer, even when that answer wins.
A material error changes what the source means, what the evidence establishes,
or what the reader can reasonably do next. Quote the relevant answer text and
cite the source line where possible. Enter None after checking an answer with
no material errors.

Fill in the scorecard and return it before consulting comparison notes or pilot
annotations. Record the reviewer name or ID and review date. The reviewer should
be independent of the case design and answer generation.

## Pair 01

### Prompt and sources

```text
Clarify whether promotion was promised.
The participant wants to understand the commitment and what they might do next. Review as of 29 October 2026.
Use only the supplied material.

L1: Manager email, 2026-10-01: "If the trial meets the agreed targets, I will put your promotion forward at the November panel."
L2: Policy excerpt, 2026-09-01: "Only the panel approves promotions."
```

### Answer A

> As of 29 October 2026, the manager had made a conditional promise to put your promotion forward. Approval rested with the panel. (L1–L2)
>
> On 1 October, the manager wrote, “If the trial meets the agreed targets, I will put your promotion forward at the November panel.” The supplied material does not specify the targets, show whether the trial met them, or give the exact panel date. (L1, manager email, 1 October 2026)
>
> The policy says, “Only the panel approves promotions.” The manager’s commitment therefore covered putting your promotion forward. (L2, policy excerpt, 1 September 2026)
>
> You could ask the manager to confirm whether the condition has been met and when the panel will consider your case:
>
> > Could you confirm whether the trial met the agreed targets? If it did, could you confirm that you’ll put my promotion forward at the November panel and let me know when I can expect a decision?

### Answer B

> As of 29 October 2026, the manager had promised to put your promotion forward at the November panel if the trial met the agreed targets. The promise covered putting your case forward. Only the panel could approve the promotion. (L1, L2)
>
> The excerpts leave open whether the trial met the targets or your case had already been submitted. Ask your manager to confirm whether the targets were met and, if so, whether your case is on the November panel’s agenda and when you can expect a decision.

## Pair 04

### Prompt and sources

```text
Review chronology-item.txt and compare it with chronology-comparison.txt for an editor deciding what the update contributes. Use only the supplied fictional excerpts.

chronology-item.txt
Quay library update — 2026-10-20, publication time unknown
L1. The Saturday opening moved to 9 a.m.
L2. The return slot now accepts oversized books.
L3. The library added a quiet study room.
L4. Visitors can reserve a study room online.
L5. This change will halve no-shows.
L6. The October booking export recorded 24 no-shows out of 120 bookings before online reservations began.

chronology-comparison.txt
Staff bulletin — 2026-10-18
L1. Saturday doors will open at 9 a.m.

Community newsletter — 2026-10-22
L1. The return slot takes oversized books.

Room guide — date unknown
L1. There is a new quiet study room at the library.

Booking notice — 2026-10-20, publication time and ordering unknown
L1. Visitors can reserve study rooms online.
```

### Answer A

> The update’s clearest factual contribution is the reported pre-launch baseline of **24 no-shows in 120 bookings (20%)**. It also gives the earliest dated account of the oversized-book return slot in the supplied excerpts. The claim that online reservations will halve no-shows is a forecast.
>
> | Update line | What it contributes |
> |---|---|
> | L1 · Saturday opening at 9 a.m. | The October 18 staff bulletin had already announced the 9 a.m. opening. The October 20 update reports the change as having taken effect. |
> | L2 · Oversized books in the return slot | The October 20 update reports this two days before the October 22 community newsletter. |
> | L3 · New quiet study room | The undated room guide reports the same addition. Either account could have come first. |
> | L4 · Online study room reservations | The booking notice reports the same service on October 20. Publication times and order are unknown. |
> | L5 · No-shows will halve | The update adds a prediction about online reservations. Testing it requires post-launch booking and no-show figures. |
> | L6 · Pre-launch no-shows | The October booking export provides a baseline that appears only in the update. Halving the 20% rate would bring it to 10%, or 12 no-shows at the same volume of 120 bookings. |

### Answer B

> The update’s main contribution is a reported pre-launch no-show rate of 20%. It also adds a forecast that online reservations will halve no-shows. The supplied evidence establishes the arithmetic behind the baseline and leaves the forecast unresolved.
>
> 1. **The forecast has insufficient evidence.** “This change will halve no-shows” appears immediately after the announcement of online reservations. It leaves the timeframe unspecified and could refer to either the count or the rate. The excerpts provide no post-launch figures or analysis linking online reservations to a reduction. (Quay library update, 2026-10-20, L4–L6.)
> 2. **The reported baseline is concrete.** The update attributes “24 no-shows out of 120 bookings” to the October booking export before online reservations began. That works out to 20%. Halving the rate would bring it to 10%, or 12 no-shows at the same booking volume. The export itself was unavailable for verification. (Quay library update, 2026-10-20, L6.)
>
> ### Comparison
>
> - **Overlap.** The 9 a.m. Saturday opening was announced before the update (Staff bulletin, 2026-10-18, L1). The oversized-book claim overlaps with later coverage (Community newsletter, 2026-10-22, L1). Priority is unknown for the quiet study room (Room guide, undated, L1) and online reservations (Booking notice, 2026-10-20, L1, publication order unknown).
> - **Added.** Within the supplied comparison set, the update adds the reported booking figures and the halving forecast. The figures quantify the baseline. (Quay library update, L5–L6.)
> - **Unchecked.** The review was limited to the supplied excerpts. The four service changes were compared across excerpts, and their implementation was not independently checked.
>
> What no-show count and total booking count did the library record in a comparable period after online reservations began?

Use only the supplied material.

L1: Screenshot fragment, date unknown: "There will be consequences."

The fragment leaves open what would trigger the consequences, what they would be, and whether the speaker has any authority to impose them. (L1–L2)
>
> - If you can identify the speaker and feel comfortable replying, ask: “What consequences are you referring to, and what would trigger them?”

## Pair 06

### Prompt and sources

```text
Explain this test result.
Explain it to a product manager deciding whether to promise response times.
Use only the supplied material.

L1: The staging report defines p95 as the smallest response time at or below which at least 95% of the 2,000 measured requests completed; p95 was 720 ms.
L2: Production traffic was not tested.
```

### Answer A

> In staging, at least 1,900 of the 2,000 measured requests completed in 720 ms or less. Up to 100 requests took longer.
>
> Use 720 ms as a staging benchmark. Production traffic was untested, so this result alone doesn’t support promising customers that 95% of production requests will complete within 720 ms.

### Answer B

> In staging, at least 1,900 of the 2,000 measured requests completed in 720 ms or less. The **p95 was 720 ms**, meaning that 720 ms was the shortest response-time cutoff that covered at least 95% of those requests. (L1)
>
> Hold off on promising customers a 720 ms p95. The test did not include production traffic, so the report leaves production response times unknown. (L2)

## Pair 08

### Prompt and sources

```text
Explain what cannot yet be compared.
Explain it to a procurement coordinator comparing throughput offers.
Use only the supplied material.

L1: Supplier A quotes throughput of 50 units per hour without defining a unit.
L2: Supplier B quotes 40 completed parcels per hour under a specified packing workload.
```

### Answer A

> You can’t yet tell which supplier completes more parcels per hour under Supplier B’s specified packing workload. Supplier A quotes 50 units per hour without defining a unit, while Supplier B quotes 40 completed parcels per hour under that workload.
>
> For a like-for-like comparison, ask Supplier A to quote its throughput in completed parcels per hour under the same packing workload.

### Answer B

> You can’t yet tell which supplier completes more parcels per hour. Supplier A quotes **50 units per hour** but doesn’t define a unit (L1). Supplier B quotes **40 completed parcels per hour** under a specified packing workload (L2).
>
> Ask Supplier A whether each unit is a completed parcel and what rate it quotes under Supplier B’s specified workload. That would give you a common basis for comparing the offers.
