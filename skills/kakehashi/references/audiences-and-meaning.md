# Audiences and meaning

## Adapt to the task

Ask who needs to understand the material if no audience is specified. Inspecting
the source can proceed while waiting; selecting an audience cannot. Use supplied
knowledge and purpose rather than stereotypes about titles or education.

| Audience task | Useful emphasis | Preserve |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Implement or investigate | Mechanism, definitions, inputs, failure conditions, and unresolved design choices. | Exact constraints and the distinction between requirements and proposals. |
| Decide or allocate resources | What was observed, what decision it informs, and what remains unknown. | Test setting, uncertainty, costs actually supplied, and decision authority. |
| Understand or act without specialist background | Define essential terms beside their practical meaning; give a concrete next step when supported. | Exceptions and limits that would change that step. |

These are task prompts, not mandatory templates. Do not remove a useful term
merely because it is uncommon. Define a term by its role: a median describes the
middle of ordered observations, not their arithmetic average. Preserve the
source's definition when conventions differ or details are incomplete.

## Requirements are not acceptance criteria

For “Staff must be able to export monthly totals,” the export capability is
stated. A file format, deadline, performance threshold, access policy, and
rounding rule are not supplied. A useful interpretation separates:

- Stated requirement: export monthly totals.
- Proposed acceptance criterion: compare exported totals with an agreed sample
  month. This is a suggested check, pending agreement.
- Unresolved choices: which staff, which totals, format, and rounding rules.

Do not turn this example into a universal export specification. Preserve a
precise supplied criterion when one exists. An ambiguity is a question to resolve,
not permission to fill in an implementation or business benefit.

## Analogies have boundaries

Use an analogy only if it explains a specific relationship. Say where it stops
working beside the analogy, before readers might act on it.

Example: “A cache is like keeping a nearby copy of a reference sheet to avoid
fetching it again. The copy can become outdated; this analogy does not promise
that it matches the current original.” If the source gives a refresh interval,
retain it. If it does not, leave freshness unresolved rather than inventing one.
Do not use an analogy as evidence of security, reliability, causality, or safety.

## Check meaning and action

Trace each source proposition to the explanation. Check actor, attribution,
quantity, denominator, units, time period, population, modality, condition, and
negation. Trace new assertions in the other direction; proposed suggestions must
remain visibly proposed. A conditional outcome is not a guarantee. A measured
association does not establish an intervention's effect. An incomplete report
cannot establish what its missing pages contain.

Ask a question with an assessable answer when understanding matters: “Does this
test establish production response time?” is better than “Does that make sense?”
Check factual understanding, scope/uncertainty, and the relevant decision or next
action separately. Seeking evidence or declining to act may be correct. Do not
judge readers solely by agreement with the explanation or exact wording.

## Evidence and limits

These are original design safeguards, not measured Kakehashi effects. The
foundation's review of [Cruz and Lombrozo (2025)](https://cognition.princeton.edu/sites/g/files/toruqf3386/files/documents/s41562-025-02227-0.pdf)
covered the abstract, Results/preregistration, and Studies 3A–4 discussion on
pp. 1–2. It recorded empirical evidence distinguishing explanation satisfaction
from comprehension and a role for follow-up questions. This phase does not
claim a new literature review. Transfer to AI-assisted workplace explanations
is uncertain; neither blanket jargon removal nor meaning preservation by this
skill is established. Analogies and the requirements split above are design
choices, not separately validated interventions.

Examples are original synthetic illustrations. Independent annotations and
reader comprehension/action tests are still required; model self-ratings and
structural checks cannot substitute for them.
